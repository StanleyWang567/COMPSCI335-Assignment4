About

New Zealand Sign Language (NZSL), or Te Reo Turi, is one of Aotearoa New Zealand's three official languages. NZSL is a rich and expressive language used by Deaf people and the wider Deaf community throughout New Zealand.

This application has been designed to help beginners take their first steps in learning NZSL. Explore a selection of signs, practise your vocabulary, discover upcoming events, and connect with other learners through the guest book.

Signs

Kia Ora
Kia ora is a friendly Māori greeting commonly used throughout Aotearoa New Zealand. It can be used to say hello, acknowledge someone, or express thanks. In an NZSL learning context, it provides a useful introduction to everyday greetings and to the relationship between NZSL and Te Reo Māori.
Mōrena
Mōrena is a Māori greeting meaning "good morning". It is commonly used as a warm and informal greeting at the beginning of the day. Learning greetings such as Mōrena gives beginners some useful vocabulary for starting simple conversations in NZSL.
You
You is a useful introductory pronoun for NZSL learners and can be used when addressing another person. Pronouns are particularly interesting in NZSL because the signing space can be used to identify and refer to people. This makes simple words such as "you" a good starting point for exploring how NZSL communicates meaning visually.
I/Me
I/Me Me refers to the person who is signing and is another useful introductory pronoun. Learning "I/me" alongside "you" allows beginners to start constructing simple interactions and conversations. These everyday words also provide an opportunity to explore how meaning is expressed through the use of space in NZSL.
